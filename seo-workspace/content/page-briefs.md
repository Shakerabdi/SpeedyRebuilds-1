## Homepage: existing `/`

Searcher problem: finding a local person to build or improve a website. Keep design, website-designer and web-developer synonyms together on this page; do not create three competing pages for them. The provider and local intent support one commercial page, although redirect-only organic SERP output prevents a complete URL-overlap test.

Cover who the studio works with, website design and rebuilds, real examples, the process, quoting, ownership, ongoing help, actual service area, and direct contact. The code now makes that focus explicit while retaining creators, nonprofits, independent projects and remote Canadian clients.

Link to a future redesign page when it has distinct useful content and to actual case studies when permission and source material are ready. Do not create a separate Windsor design page without intentionally reassigning the homepage's target.

## Redesign: proposed `/website-redesign/`

Searcher problem: deciding whether an existing website needs a focused refresh or a full rebuild. Metrics for the exact local term are unknown, so prioritize business fit and inquiry evidence rather than an invented volume estimate.

Sections: when a refresh is enough; when a rebuild is justified; what to preserve; content and URL inventory; redirects and search continuity; mobile and contact-flow checks; scope and quoting; an approved example; realistic process; FAQ; request an audit. Keep timelines and pricing scoped to an actual quote. Link back to homepage, audit/contact and the relevant real project.

Suggested H1: “Website redesigns for Windsor-Essex businesses.” Suggested description: “Refresh an outdated website with clearer content, mobile-friendly pages and easier contact options. Start with a free website review from SpeedyRebuilds.”

## Audit: current `/#contact`, separate page proposed later

Searcher problem: understanding what to fix before paying for a redesign. The national `free website audit` query is competitive and often tool-oriented. Present a human review, its actual scope, example observations, and the next step without promising rankings or automated scores.

Sections for a future page: who the review helps; homepage/mobile/contact/search checks; what the owner receives; a truthful sample using an owned or authorized site; privacy; name/email/website fields with optional context. Do not promise a delivery deadline or unlimited free work unless the owner agrees.

The local code now makes the hero audit link select the correct service and avoids requiring a project essay for a review. A separate route is not needed for that conversion improvement.

## Maintenance and landing pages: later

Create these only when the owner has confirmed the offer and content. Maintenance needs a precise inclusion/exclusion list, update process, account ownership, and agreed recurring costs. Landing-page content should explain one offer, one audience and a measurable inquiry action, with approved examples. Exact local demand remains unknown.

## Case studies: next proof asset

Use an actual completed project, the customer's permission, the starting problem, real screenshots, delivered changes, and an honest explanation of the result. If traffic, sales or inquiry measurements do not exist, describe deliverables instead of inventing outcomes. Link each study to the most relevant service and inquiry CTA. Do not present a demo as a real client's commissioned work.

## Publishing constraints

The current application has no routing layer for these proposed pages. A future implementation needs real URLs, unique metadata, working direct navigation, correct HTTP behavior, internal links, and sitemap entries. Section fragments are not separate pages. Avoid adding unimplemented paths to the sitemap or generating repetitive town-name pages.
