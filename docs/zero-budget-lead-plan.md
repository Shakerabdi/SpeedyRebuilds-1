# SpeedyRebuilds: a first month with two days a week

Prepared November 8, 2026. Marketing budget: $0. Time available: two days each week. Existing inquiry volume is unknown, so this is a first-month experiment rather than a forecast.

Aim for 1–2 qualified inquiries in 30 days. A qualified inquiry means an owner asks about paid website help and provides a way to continue the conversation. A researched business, social like, free audit request, and paying customer are different milestones.

## The offer

Start with Windsor-Essex service businesses. Your site already features a plumbing project, which gives this campaign a relevant example. Confirm the client's permission before using its name or screenshots in promotional material.

Use one invitation:

> Get a free three-point website review. We’ll look at your mobile experience, the clarity of your services, and how easily customers can contact you. You’ll receive three practical recommendations, with no obligation to buy.

Limit each free review to 20 minutes. Deliver three observations and a next step. Offer a small, clearly scoped improvement when that solves the problem. Recommend a rebuild only when the owner's goals justify it. Avoid building an entire free website before someone expresses interest.

## Your two working days

The hours below assume about four focused hours on each available day. Move the blocks to fit your schedule; reserve additional time for paid delivery.

| Day | Time | Work | Weekly output to aim for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | 30 min | Check the inbox and tracker. Respond to inquiries and identify the next action. | Every active opportunity has a next step. |
| Day 1 | 60 min | Review six local businesses. Keep the best three based on a specific, verified website issue and a credible route to a conversation. | Three researched prospects. |
| Day 1 | 60 min | Have conversations through existing contacts, introductions, and relevant local business communities. Ask for introductions rather than purchased lists. | Three relevant conversations to work toward. |
| Day 1 | 45 min | Publish one useful website tip or approved project example on your business profile or a community that allows promotion. Invite owners to request the review. | One useful post with one clear invitation. |
| Day 1 | 45 min | Improve one sales asset: an approved client story, proposal template, or audit example. | One reusable asset. |
| Day 2 | 30 min | Check messages and follow up where a review or quote was requested. | No forgotten requested follow-up. |
| Day 2 | 60 min | Deliver up to two requested reviews, including writing and explaining the findings. | Up to two completed reviews when requested. |
| Day 2 | 60 min | Have discovery conversations or prepare quotes for interested owners. If none are waiting, use this time for introductions and helpful participation. | Two more relevant conversations to work toward. |
| Day 2 | 60 min | Work on paid delivery, client care, or a permission-approved case study. | Progress on delivery and proof of work. |
| Day 2 | 30 min | Record new interest, next dates, and the source of every opportunity. Review which activity produced replies. | One updated tracker and a clear plan for next week. |

This totals eight hours of scheduled blocks across two days. Treat five relevant conversations per week as an activity target. It is not a known conversion benchmark. At the end of the month, compare the actual conversations, review requests, paid-work inquiries, quotes, and wins.

If possible, spend five minutes checking messages on other weekdays. If that is not possible, make your reply-time promise match your availability. Your current website says you usually respond within one business day.

## First four weeks

1. **Week 1:** Check that a real inquiry reaches your inbox and is recorded. Fix the misleading error behavior described below. Prepare the three-point audit template and enter existing genuine opportunities in the tracker. Ask the clients behind your featured projects for feedback and, if appropriate, an introduction.
2. **Week 2:** Work the two-day routine. Share one approved project example and deliver requested reviews. Record where each conversation began.
3. **Week 3:** Repeat the routine. Follow up on requested reviews and quotes. Ask interested owners about their priorities, budget range, decision-maker, and timing before estimating work.
4. **Week 4:** Count results. If you had few conversations, improve introductions and community participation. If conversations produced no review requests, test the invitation. If reviews produced no paid-work interest, reconsider fit and the proposed next step. Continue the activity that produced actual interest.

## Draft words you can use

### Spoken introduction

> I run SpeedyRebuilds. I help local businesses make their websites clearer and easier for customers to use. I offer a short review with three practical improvements. What would you most like your website to do better?

### Spoken referral request to a satisfied client

> I’m taking on a small number of website projects. Do you know another business owner who has mentioned needing website help? If you’re comfortable, could you ask whether they’d like an introduction?

### Public post, where promotion is allowed

> Windsor-Essex business owners: can a new customer tell what you offer and how to contact you from their phone? I run SpeedyRebuilds and offer a free three-point website review covering mobile usability, clear service information, and contacting your business. If you’d like a review, request one at https://speedyrebuilds.com/#contact and select “Free website audit.”

> contact@speedyrebuilds.com | 226-507-5802

> To stop further marketing messages from SpeedyRebuilds, reply “unsubscribe.”

Replace the placeholders and confirm the recipient's request before using the draft. For electronic sales messages, establish a valid consent basis and include the required sender identification, contact details, and unsubscribe method. An electronic request for marketing consent can itself be a commercial message. See the [CRTC FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm) and [guidance on implied consent](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm). The plan centers on inbound requests and introductions made with permission.

### One follow-up after a requested review or quote

> Hi [Name], following up on the [review/quote] you requested. Is [their stated priority] still something you want help with? I’m happy to clarify the proposed next step. If the timing has changed, let me know and I’ll close this out.

Use the same identification and unsubscribe footer for marketing follow-ups. Agree on a follow-up date when possible. If there is no reply, make one follow-up after about five business days, then stop unless they invite further contact. Honor any request to stop immediately.

## Protect your time and project income

- Use a short discovery call to establish the business goal, the decision-maker, timing, budget range, and who supplies text and photographs.
- Put the scope in writing: pages, forms, content responsibilities, revisions, launch work, ownership, hosting, recurring costs, exclusions, and payment milestones. Confirm actual terms before promising them.
- Estimate delivery hours and third-party costs before quoting. Agree on payment and any deposit before starting work. Do not assume a low price is required because your own marketing budget is zero.
- Give every active opportunity a next action and date. Use the tracker rather than trying to remember conversations across social messages and email.
- Ask for honest feedback after a successful handover. Get approval for public testimonials and screenshots. Publish measurable client results only when you have records supporting them.

## Website priorities

1. **Reliable inquiry handling:** the local submission code sends the email before saving to the database. A later database failure can show the visitor an error after an email has been sent. Separate the delivery outcomes. During browser work on November 8, an internal test returned the success message and the matching email arrived in the business inbox. A separate database-console check and failure-path repair remain outstanding.
2. **A shorter audit request:** test a focused flow asking for a name, email, website, and optional goal. The general project form can continue collecting broader details.
3. **Trust:** develop a short, approved client story for each featured project. Use actual deliverables and honest feedback.
4. **Discovery:** verify the site in Search Console and check indexing. A sitemap helps discovery but does not guarantee indexing or rankings. [Google's sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).
5. **Local listing, if eligible:** Google Business Profile requires qualifying in-person customer contact. An online-only service is not eligible. [Google's eligibility rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en-CA).

## Using the tracker

The accompanying workbook starts with the three research candidates. It has room for 50 businesses and dates for interest, last contact, and next follow-up. It counts recorded businesses, qualified inquiries in the selected date range, and follow-ups due today or earlier.

Use `Research` while assessing a business, `Contacted` after an initial message is sent, and `Connected` after a real conversation occurs. Move to `Audit requested` when an owner requests the free review. Enter a first interest date only when the owner asks about paid website work, then use `Interested`, `Quoted`, `Won`, or `Closed` as appropriate. The interest date preserves the inquiry count even after the outcome changes.

The initial reporting period is November 9–December 8, 2026. Change the yellow start/end dates for the next period. Record a business once and add conversation details to its existing row. New rows must stay within rows 11–60 for the existing summary formulas. Keep a practical record of contact permission and the source of each opportunity in the contact and notes columns.
